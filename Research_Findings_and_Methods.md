# Self-medication among healthcare students: research findings

**University of Zambia, Ridgeway Campus — analysis for Lusale Esther**  
**Prepared:** 7 October 2026  
**Sources:** the uploaded research proposal and `SelfMedicatingPrevelanceCleaned.csv`.

## Executive summary

- **Prevalence:** 344/383 respondents reported self-medication in the past 6–12 months: **89.8%**, Wilson 95% CI **86.4%–92.5%**.
- **Common medicines:** analgesics/painkillers 312/344 (90.7%); antibiotics 169/344 (49.1%); cough syrups/decongestants 156/344 (45.3%).
- **Main driver and source:** lack of time 282/344 (82.0%); commercial pharmacies 307/344 (89.2%). These are reported reasons and access points, not proven causes.
- **Awareness:** agreement with four risk statements ranged from 87.6% to 94.5% among valid item responses, despite high self-reported practice.
- **Associations:** self-rated competence and sex met the unadjusted p<0.05 threshold. Neither remained below 0.05 after Holm adjustment across eight tests. The exploratory demographic-only adjusted model found no coefficient with p<0.05.
- **Critical caveat:** 37/39 respondents answering No still completed at least one Section B item. Their behaviour follow-ups are excluded from pattern summaries; the primary Yes/No answer is retained.

## 1. Study design, scope and proposal alignment

The proposal specifies a descriptive and analytical cross-sectional study among full-time undergraduate healthcare students at UNZA Ridgeway, with voluntary online recruitment across four schools. Its objectives are to estimate prevalence and identify associated factors. The planned study window is August–September 2026; the CSV contains no timestamps and cannot verify actual collection dates, ethics clearance, eligibility or consent. This analysis treats the supplied dataset as the observed study responses, without independently verifying authenticity.

Age is recorded as groups, not exact years. Therefore, exact mean age and standard deviation are not available. Health Sciences subdisciplines are not separated in the CSV. The overall sample is unweighted; voluntary recruitment and unknown inclusion probabilities limit generalisation to the entire campus.

Tests are two-sided at p<0.05. Whenever an expected count is below 5 in a multi-category table, a conditional Monte Carlo chi-square p-value is used (100,000 draws; seed 20261007); otherwise uncorrected Pearson chi-square is used. Holm correction across eight tests is supplemental to the proposal. No factor passes the corrected threshold; competence is borderline (adjusted p=0.0516). Failure to reject a null does not demonstrate no relationship.

Outcome: Yes=1, No=0. Reference groups: female, age 18–20, Medicine. Year of study enters as a linear per-year term (2–6). The model contains n=382, 343 Yes and 39 No; seven slope parameters; convergence confirmed. Global likelihood-ratio p=0.1002.

**Do not make strong independent-predictor claims.** There are only approximately 5.6 No outcomes per slope parameter, and Nursing Sciences has only one No, producing a very wide confidence interval. This model adjusts only for demographics, not competence, knowledge, attitude or previous experience. Odds ratios are not prevalence ratios and do not establish causality.

Cleaning trims whitespace and terminal punctuation, standardises category labels, and splits semicolon-separated selections. No records are removed for identical response patterns because none are duplicated; no values are imputed. Raw data are preserved in the workbook. Contradictory follow-ups do not overwrite the primary outcome. Driver/access responses from No respondents are not tested as exposures because their temporal and skip-logic meaning is unclear.

## 6. Recommendations for the research write-up

1. **Lead with sample prevalence and denominators.** State the 6–12-month recall window and retain the Wilson confidence interval; avoid claiming campus-wide representativeness.
2. **Prioritise rational medicine-use education.** The reported antibiotic and analgesic use suggests topics for teaching and pharmacy counselling. Do not claim measured antimicrobial resistance or adverse events.
3. **Investigate practical access barriers.** Lack of time and waiting were common self-reported reasons; explore scheduling, low-barrier consultations and student service pathways as candidate interventions, not proven solutions.
4. **Validate contradictory responses before dissertation finalisation.** Resolve scope and skip-logic errors where ethically and operationally feasible; do not silently change the supplied answers.
5. **Treat inference as exploratory.** Report both raw and adjusted p-values, uncertainty, and limited regression stability. A larger, more balanced sample would support stronger adjusted comparisons.
6. **Separate awareness from practice.** A high agreement score alone does not establish safe prescribing knowledge, correct dosage, or appropriate self-care.

## 7. Proposal issues needing reconciliation

| Topic | Review finding |
| --- | --- |
| Sampling claims | Voluntary online recruitment does not establish representative campus sampling. Report recruitment transparently and avoid campus-population prevalence claims. |
| Sample size wording | Proposal final target is 387 (school quotas sum to 387), with a 350-400 range. Observed 383 achieves 99.0% of target, not a response rate. Nonresponse adjustment by multiplying 1.10 is not equivalent to dividing by 0.90. |
| Age analysis mismatch | Methods propose mean/SD for age, but questionnaire only records age groups. Use grouped counts and percentages unless exact ages are separately available. |
| School detail | Questionnaire lists Health Sciences subdisciplines, but the CSV records only aggregate School. Pharmacy, biomedical science and allied-health comparisons cannot be supported. |
| Questionnaire skip logic | No respondents should skip Q6-Q8; 37 did not. Enforce branching and review contradictory responses. Sections D also have answers among No respondents. |
| Drug definition | Western medicinal products are the stated scope, but herbal/home-remedy free text occurs. Clarify scope in the questionnaire and follow-up validation. |
| Confidentiality / consent | Proposal requires student email/computer-number sign-in but also promises anonymity and no computer numbers recorded. Explain platform access controls and de-identification; the CSV alone does not verify consent or approval. |
| Budget consistency | Budget table totals ZMW 2,695; prose states ZMW 2,850 and a different contingency. Printing is listed as 400 x ZMW 4 but allocated ZMW 1,300. Reconcile the figures; these are proposal issues, not study findings. |
| Literature claims | Cited background prevalence figures were not verified externally and are not presented as observations from this dataset. Validate references and ensure comparable populations/recall windows before comparisons. |
